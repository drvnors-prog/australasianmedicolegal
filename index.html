Nature and intended use

This calculator produces a statistical estimate of remaining life expectancy following moderate-to-severe traumatic brain injury, based on the peer-reviewed actuarial model published in Bala (2026). It is a decision-support aid intended solely for suitably qualified health, rehabilitation and medico-legal professionals exercising their own independent judgement.

An estimate, not a prediction

Each result is a population mean for a group of people sharing the entered characteristics. It is not a prediction of any individual's lifespan and is not a guarantee. The life expectancy of any particular person may differ substantially from the estimate.

Not advice; no professional relationship

The calculator does not provide medical, clinical, legal or financial advice. Use of it does not create any clinician–patient or practitioner–client relationship, and it is not a substitute for individual clinical assessment or independent expert opinion.

Known limitations

The model does not account for factors including problematic substance use, psychiatric comorbidity or post-injury seizures; it is derived from United States cohorts of inpatient-rehabilitation survivors and predates the COVID-19 pandemic; its functional categories are broad; and the severity (SMR) mode is a screening approximation only. Results must be interpreted in light of these limitations.

Read before use — this is an estimate, not a prediction

  • The figure is a population mean for a group of similar patients. It is not a guaranteed lifespan for any one person and must be presented as such.
  • The model omits several known mortality modifiers, including problematic alcohol or drug use, psychiatric comorbidity, and post-injury seizures. Adjust by clinical judgement for the specific case.
  • Functional categories are broad. The "does not walk, fed by others" group overstates survival in the most extreme neurological states (prolonged disorders of consciousness, gastrostomy dependence); "walks well" is pessimistic for people living wholly independently.
  • Parameters derive from United States moderate-to-severe TBI cohorts of inpatient-rehabilitation survivors, and predate the COVID-19 pandemic. Extrapolation to other health systems, untreated injury, or uncomplicated mild TBI is uncertain.
  • The severity (SMR) mode is a screening approximation only, assuming constant relative risk across age. Prefer the functional method where FIM data exist.
  • Intended as a structured aid to — not a replacement for — individualised clinical judgement. Pair every estimate with its caveats and, where required, a standard error.

About the practitioner

A rehabilitation physician with three decades at the bench and the bedside.

Independent, impartial and grounded in active clinical and academic practice.

Associate Professor Vaidya Bala is an accomplished rehabilitation physician whose practice spans clinical care, academic medicine, teaching and health leadership across Australia and New Zealand.

He currently serves as Medical Co-Director of Population and Public Health and Senior Staff Specialist in Rehabilitation Medicine with the Illawarra and Shoalhaven Local Health District, and as Conjoint Associate Professor with the University of Wollongong. He is a senior examiner for the FAFRM (RACP) Court of Examiners.

As a Certified Independent Medical Examiner, he provides accredited permanent impairment assessment under the AMA Guides (4th, 5th and 6th editions). His clinical expertise covers acquired and traumatic brain injury, stroke and neurological rehabilitation, movement disorders and spasticity, functional neurological disorder, and chronic pain — informed by statewide brain injury services and more than twenty years of consultant practice.

His opinions are independent of any treating relationship and prepared to assist the referrer and, where relevant, the court — clear, evidence-based and balanced.

Virtual consultation

Medico-legal assessment by secure video, wherever the matter sits.

Convenient for regional referrers and examinees, without compromising rigour.

  1. Referral & brief

    Send the letter of instruction, specific questions and supporting records by email. Suitability for a virtual assessment is confirmed on review.

  2. Scheduled video examination

    The examinee attends a confidential video consultation at an arranged time, from home or the instructing office, on a secure platform.

  3. Independent report

    A structured, evidence-based report is prepared addressing each question, with impairment ratings provided where requested.

Is a virtual assessment suitable?

Telehealth examination is well suited to many file reviews, supplementary opinions and assessments where a detailed physical examination is not central to the question.

Drawing on published work in benefit–risk frameworks for virtual health care, each referral is screened so that matters genuinely requiring in-person examination are identified early and arranged at the Shellharbour consulting suites.
